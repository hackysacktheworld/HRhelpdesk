# Security Incident Response Policy

## 1. Purpose
This policy defines how employees should report suspected security incidents and how incident responders should coordinate triage, containment, investigation, communication, and closure.

## 2. Security Incident Examples
Potential incidents include compromised credentials, unauthorized system access, phishing attempts, malware, exposed customer data, lost devices containing company data, suspicious admin activity, source code leakage, and accidental disclosure of Confidential or Restricted data.

## 3. Employee Reporting Duties
Employees must report suspected incidents immediately through the security hotline or incident reporting channel. Reports should include what happened, when it was discovered, systems or data involved, people affected, and any evidence such as screenshots, messages, or logs.

Employees should not investigate beyond their role, delete evidence, contact suspected attackers, or make external statements without approval.

## 4. Triage
Security Operations assigns an incident severity based on business impact, data sensitivity, scope, active exploitation, customer impact, and regulatory concern. High-severity incidents require an incident lead, communications owner, technical lead, and legal contact.

## 5. Containment
Containment actions may include disabling accounts, rotating credentials, isolating systems, blocking network traffic, revoking tokens, suspending integrations, or disabling vendor access.

Containment should preserve evidence where practical and must be coordinated with the incident lead for high-severity incidents.

## 6. Communication
Internal incident updates should be factual, time-stamped, and shared in the approved incident channel. External customer, regulator, or media communications require approval from Legal and Communications.

Employees may acknowledge receipt of a customer security concern but must not confirm breach status, scope, root cause, or notification obligations without approval.

## 7. Recovery
Recovery activities include restoring service, verifying system integrity, applying patches, validating access controls, and monitoring for recurring activity.

## 8. Post-Incident Review
For high-severity incidents, the incident lead must complete a post-incident review within 10 business days. The review should include timeline, root cause, customer impact, controls that worked, controls that failed, corrective actions, and action owners.
