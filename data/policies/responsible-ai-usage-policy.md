# Responsible AI Usage Policy

## 1. Purpose
This policy defines acceptable use of generative AI tools for company work. It is designed to help employees use AI productively while protecting confidential information, customer trust, intellectual property, and regulatory obligations.

## 2. Approved AI Tools
Employees may use company-approved AI tools for drafting, summarizing, brainstorming, coding assistance, data exploration, and internal productivity. Approved tools are listed in the internal software catalog. Employees may not use personal AI accounts for company work unless explicitly authorized.

## 3. Restricted Data
Employees must not enter confidential company information, customer personal data, payment data, authentication secrets, employee records, legal privileged information, or non-public financial information into unapproved AI tools.

Customer personal data may be processed with an approved AI system only when the use case has completed privacy review and the minimum necessary data is used.

## 4. Human Review
AI-generated output must be reviewed by a qualified employee before it is shared externally, used in customer-impacting workflows, or relied upon for financial, legal, security, hiring, medical, or compliance decisions.

Employees are responsible for validating accuracy, tone, citations, source quality, and business fit. AI output should not be treated as authoritative without review.

## 5. Prohibited Uses
Employees may not use AI tools to make final employment decisions, produce deceptive content, impersonate people, bypass security controls, generate malware, violate intellectual property rights, or create content that discriminates against protected classes.

## 6. Citation and Source Requirements
When AI output summarizes policy, legal, technical, or customer-facing information, the employee must verify the output against approved sources. If a response includes factual claims, the final version should cite source material where practical.

## 7. Code and Technical Work
AI-generated code must go through the same review, testing, and security processes as human-authored code. Developers should not paste secrets, private keys, production credentials, or proprietary algorithms into unapproved AI tools.

## 8. Incident Reporting
Employees must report suspected AI misuse, data exposure, harmful output, or inaccurate customer-impacting AI output to Security and Legal within one business day. Urgent security issues should follow the Security Incident Response Policy.

## 9. Exceptions
Exceptions require written approval from the AI Governance Committee. An exception must include the business purpose, data types involved, risk controls, owner, and expiration date.
