# Employee Leave and Time Off Policy

## 1. Purpose
This policy explains how employees request time off, which types of leave are available, and what approvals or documentation may be required. The company supports planned rest, personal time, family needs, medical leave, and legally protected leave while maintaining business coverage.

## 2. Time Off Types
Common time off types include vacation, sick time, personal days, bereavement leave, jury duty, company holidays, parental leave, medical leave, and unpaid leave. Eligibility may depend on employment status, location, tenure, local law, and the employee's benefits plan.

## 3. How To Request Time Off
Employees should submit time off requests through the HR system as early as practical. The request should include the dates requested, type of time off, expected return date, and any coverage notes needed by the manager.

For planned vacation or personal days, employees should submit requests at least 10 business days before the first day away when possible. Managers should respond within five business days.

## 4. Manager Approval
Most vacation and personal time off requests require manager approval. Managers review requests based on business coverage, team schedules, prior commitments, and fairness across the team. Managers should not deny time off for arbitrary or discriminatory reasons.

Sick time, protected leave, jury duty, and other legally protected absences may have different approval rules. Managers should contact People Operations before denying or questioning a protected leave request.

## 5. Sick Time
Employees should report sick time as soon as reasonably possible through the HR system or by notifying their manager. If the employee is unable to submit the request immediately, they should do so when they are able.

Medical documentation may be requested only when allowed by law and company policy. Managers should not ask employees to share private medical details.

## 6. Extended Leave
Leave longer than five consecutive business days may require People Operations review. Extended leave may include medical leave, parental leave, caregiver leave, unpaid leave, or other protected leave.

Employees considering extended leave should contact People Operations before submitting the request when possible. People Operations will explain eligibility, documentation requirements, pay impact, benefits impact, and return-to-work expectations.

## 7. Coverage Planning
Employees should coordinate coverage for urgent responsibilities before planned time off. Coverage notes may include key deadlines, customer commitments, handoff contacts, and tasks that should wait until the employee returns.

Managers are responsible for approving coverage plans and making sure essential work is assigned appropriately.

## 8. Changing or Canceling Time Off
Employees may change or cancel a pending time off request in the HR system before it is approved. If the request has already been approved, the employee should notify their manager and update the HR system.

If business needs require changing approved time off, the manager should discuss the situation with the employee and People Operations before making changes.

## 9. Unplanned Absences
Employees should notify their manager as soon as practical when an unplanned absence occurs. Repeated unplanned absences may require a conversation with the manager and People Operations, unless the absence is legally protected or covered by approved accommodation.

## 10. Questions and Exceptions
Employees with questions about balances, eligibility, protected leave, documentation, or exceptions should contact People Operations. Exceptions must be documented and approved by People Operations.
